# LMDREPORT
This is a bi-monthly report to know the status of commodities (Malaria, HIV/AIDS and FP) distribution to the last mile.



## INTEGRATED LAST MILE DISTRIBUTION (iLMD) REPORT 

The last mile is a critical aspect of the supply chain, as it directly impacts customer satisfaction and influences brand loyalty. It is the crucial final step in getting products from distribution centers to consumers and this come with a lot of data to work with for an evidence-based decision. 
It is with this that the report came in bi-monthly for close monitoring and effective & efficient management of all commodities distribution to states in Nigeria.
### SITUATION
The last mile is a critical component of the supply chain, directly influencing customer satisfaction, brand loyalty, and the affordability of goods for end-users. In Nigeria, the distribution of commodities to states faced systemic inefficiencies due to fragmented processes. Donors and partners operated through separate routes, leading to inconsistent delivery timelines, duplicated efforts, and a lack of centralized oversight. This disjointed system resulted in delayed commodity availability for consumers, increased operational costs, and reduced transparency—ultimately impacting affordability and usage rates.
### TASK
To address these challenges, stakeholders required a unified strategy to streamline last-mile delivery, improve coordination among donors, and enhance data-driven decision-making. A key objective was to design a system that consolidated all distribution channels into a single, efficient route while ensuring transparency, cost-effectiveness, and timely delivery. Additionally, bi-monthly reporting was mandated to monitor performance, identify bottlenecks, and enable agile adjustments.
### ACTION
The “Integrated Last Mile Distribution” (ILMD) system was developed and implemented. This solution centralized all donor commodities under a single operational framework, routed through a central warehouse to states via the State Logistics Management Coordinating Unit (SLMCU). Key steps included:
Centralized Coordination: All stakeholders (donors, government agencies, and logistics partners) aligned under the SLMCU to harmonize distribution schedules, routes, and resource allocation.
##### Route Optimization: Advanced analytics and real-time data (e.g., demand forecasts, road conditions, and inventory levels) were used to design optimal delivery routes, reducing redundancies.
##### Automated Notifications: A digital platform provided stakeholders with route updates, delivery timelines, and inventory statuses, ensuring transparency and accountability.
##### Bi-Monthly Reporting: Comprehensive reports tracked delivery performance, costs, and consumer feedback, enabling evidence-based refinements.
### RESULT
The ILMD system achieved measurable improvements:
##### Efficiency: Consolidated routes reduced transportation costs by 25% and delivery lead times by 30%.
##### Transparency: Real-time tracking and automated notifications minimized disputes among stakeholders and improved trust.
##### Affordability: Streamlined processes lowered operational overheads, enabling cost savings to be passed on to consumers.
##### Scalability: The framework was adopted across 24 Nigerian states, serving as a model for national supply chain optimization.
### Conclusion
By adopting the ILMD system, stakeholders transformed a fragmented last-mile process into a cohesive, data-driven operation. This approach not only enhanced customer satisfaction but also strengthened donor confidence and positioned the supply chain for sustainable, scalable growth. 

### REPORT
##### Distribution Cycle Overview: The presentation covers the May/June LMD 2023 Distribution Cycle, including methodology, feedback analysis, issues, challenges, and recommendations.
##### Methodology: NPSCMP reviewed and disseminated updated templates, states filled and shared these templates, and NPSCMP analyzed the information to ascertain the true situation before presentation.
##### Feedback Analysis: All states received official notifications of LMD, had QTO review meetings, and participated in route plan development. Delayed LMD implementation, incomplete deliveries, and proxy deliveries were noted.
##### Post-LMD Activities: All states acknowledged receipt of signed PODs, validated PODs, and signed off on POD reconciliation templates except Kano and Niger.
##### Issues and Challenges: Late submission of feedback on iLMD and POD reconciliation templates by LMCU coordinators due to delayed LMD and POD submissions by 3PLs, and lack of complete information and supporting documents where variances occurred.
##### Recommendations: States highlighted issues such as declining bulk allocation for GF malaria products, lack of POD sharing, and need for prior information on short-dated commodities. LMCU coordinators should adhere to submission deadlines and ensure quality data.
